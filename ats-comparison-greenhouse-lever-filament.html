Which ATS Should a Pre-Seed to Series B Startup Actually Use?

A recruiter's honest take on Greenhouse, Lever, and Filament for early-stage teams. What you actually need at your stage, what each platform does well, and how to choose without paying for capability you'll never use.

By Matt Anders, Founder · VoltForce · July 2026
First Things First

First, what's an ATS?

If you haven't set one up before, the acronym does most of the heavy lifting. ATS stands for Applicant Tracking System. It's the software you use to run your hiring process end to end.

  1. Post job openings. To your careers page, LinkedIn, Indeed, and whatever job boards matter for your sector.
  2. Track every candidate. Whether they applied through a job posting or got sourced by a recruiter, the ATS holds their resume, contact info, notes, and current stage.
  3. Move candidates through your pipeline. Applied → screened → interviewed → offered → hired. Drag-and-drop in most modern platforms, with the stage history logged automatically.
  4. Capture interview feedback in one place. Scorecards, structured ratings, free-text notes. Stops the forwarded-email thread that nobody can find later.
  5. Generate and store offer letters. Plus signed contracts, NDAs, and the onboarding documents that you'll wish you had organized when you scale.
  6. Give the founder and hiring managers a single dashboard. Everything in flight, across every open role, real-time, without a 30-minute status meeting.

One thing to know about LinkedIn before you assume it's the answer: posting jobs there isn't free, and it isn't cheap. LinkedIn Job Slots typically run several hundred dollars per slot per month, and the integration quality between LinkedIn and various ATSes varies a lot. Greenhouse and Lever have native LinkedIn integration (which costs extra on the LinkedIn side). Smaller platforms often require manual reposting and miss applicants entirely. Worth checking which side your ATS falls on before you commit, because the cost of LinkedIn plus a poorly-integrated ATS adds up faster than founders expect.

Think of an ATS as the CRM for hiring. Same shape: pipeline view, stage-based workflow, contact records, activity history. Where it diverges from a sales CRM is in the use cases that matter for recruiting: scorecard-based interview feedback, structured offer workflows, careers-page hosting, and (in modern platforms) sector-aware candidate matching.

If you're at Pre-Seed and your hiring is happening through warm intros, you can run this off a Google Sheet for a while. The minute you're juggling 10+ active candidates across 3+ roles, the spreadsheet stops scaling and an ATS becomes the system of record. The question this article answers is which one to pick when that moment hits.

The Wrong Question

Most founders Google "best ATS" and get the answer right for the wrong company.

Most early-stage founders I talk to who are picking an ATS make the same first move. They Google "best ATS for startups," land on a comparison article written for a 500-person engineering org, and start evaluating Greenhouse or Lever based on features they'll never use.

Wrong question. The right one: what does my company actually need from an ATS at my stage? Pre-Seed needs are different from Seed. Seed is different from Series A. Series A is different from Series B. And whether you're running searches yourself or working with a recruiter changes the answer too.

This article is a recruiter's take on the three platforms most relevant to early-stage startups in climatech, advanced materials, and Industry 4.0: Greenhouse, Lever, and Filament. I have a horse in the race because Filament is the platform we built at VoltForce. I'll be honest about what each one is actually good for, and how to think about the decision at your stage.

At This Stage

Pre-Seed to Series B teams need 80% less ATS than they think.

Strip the marketing back and an ATS at Pre-Seed to Series B mostly has to do five things well.

  1. Store candidate records. Resume, notes, contact info, current stage. That's it.
  2. Show a simple pipeline view per role. Sourced → screened → interview → offer → hired. Drag-and-drop. No 17 sub-stages.
  3. Capture interview feedback from the team without making it a chore. If your engineers won't fill out a feedback form, the system has lost.
  4. Generate or attach offer letters and store signed copies. The legal record matters when you scale.
  5. Show what's actually happening across all open roles without a 30-minute meeting. One dashboard. Real-time. Founder, hiring manager, and interviewer should all be able to read it in 30 seconds.

That's the real list. Everything else (multi-region compliance, 50 integrations, automated sourcing, enterprise reporting, AI-powered candidate scoring against arbitrary criteria) is overhead you don't need yet. If you're hiring 5 to 15 people a year, the system has to be fast, simple, and usable by the founder and two interviewers without a quarterly admin sprint.

Option 1

Greenhouse: built for the team you're not yet.

Greenhouse is the enterprise gold standard for recruiting operations. Founded in 2012. TPG Growth and The Rise Fund took a majority stake in January 2021 with a $500 million investment, and Greenhouse has remained privately held since. If you're at Series C or beyond, running 100+ open roles, with a dedicated recruiting ops team and structured interview kits, Greenhouse is genuinely excellent. It's configurable, integrates with most major HRIS and source-of-truth systems, supports complex multi-region hiring, and has one of the cleanest scorecard frameworks in the market.

At Pre-Seed to Series B, almost all of that is overhead you'll never use. The pricing reflects the target market: low-five-figures annually at minimum, scaling fast as you add seats, modules, and integrations. The setup curve is steep. Most teams I've watched stand up Greenhouse spend 6 to 10 weeks getting it configured before it actually saves them time, and then someone on the team becomes the de facto admin to keep it tuned.

For an early-stage startup hiring 5 to 20 people a year, the math doesn't work. You're paying enterprise pricing for a system optimized for hiring volume you don't have, and you're carrying the admin tax in founder hours.

Option 2

Lever: lighter setup, less roadmap clarity.

Lever launched around the same time as Greenhouse (2012) and competed with it on the same enterprise-recruiting market for the better part of a decade. Acquired by Employ Inc. in 2022, where it now sits alongside JazzHR, Jobvite, and a few other ATS brands under one corporate parent.

The product strengths: better candidate-relationship management out of the box, lighter setup than Greenhouse for teams that don't need full structured-interview machinery, decent CRM-style sourcing for inbound nurturing.

The honest weakness in 2026: less clarity on roadmap and investment priority since the Employ acquisition. Lever has plenty of customers and works fine, but it's harder to bet on where the product is headed than when Lever was running independently. Pricing sits in the same general range as Greenhouse (low-five-figures annually, scaling with seats and modules).

For Pre-Seed to Series B teams, Lever is the slightly lighter sibling of the same general category. Same trade-offs apply. You're paying enterprise pricing for capability you mostly won't use.

Decision Framework

What I'd actually pick at each stage.

Here's the framework I'd use, based on what I see early-stage teams actually need.

  1. Pre-Seed. Don't pay for any ATS. You're hiring 1 to 3 people, all of them are friend-of-friend or warm intros. A shared Notion doc, a Google Sheet, or Filament's standalone tier is all you need. The minute you have a paid ATS subscription at Pre-Seed, you're optimizing for the wrong thing.
  2. Seed. Same story, slightly more structure. If you're hiring 3 to 8 people in the next 6 months, the decision is between running it yourself (Filament standalone, light Lever tier) or partnering with a specialist recruiter on Talent Partner (Filament included). Most Seed teams I work with end up going Talent Partner once they realize the founder time cost of running it yourself is the binding constraint.
  3. Series A. Hiring 10 to 25 people in the next 18 months. The ATS question becomes real here. If you're running it yourself with an in-house recruiter, Filament standalone or Lever's lighter tier are both reasonable picks. If you're working with VoltForce on Talent Partner, Filament is included and you don't need a second platform. Greenhouse remains overkill at this stage unless you're already bringing on a head of TA who has strong Greenhouse preference and a real reason for it.
  4. Series B. Hiring 25 to 50 people in the next 18 months. This is the stage where Greenhouse starts to make sense if you're standing up a real TA function and need enterprise capability. For deeptech-adjacent companies still leaning on sector specialists for senior hires, Filament continues to fit. The choice depends on whether your hiring is volume-driven (Greenhouse) or specialist-driven (Filament).

The honest answer at any of these stages: the ATS choice matters less than picking a recruiting partner who knows your sector. The platform without the network is a database. The network with the right platform compounds.

The Bottom Line

The ATS isn't the answer. The pairing is.

Most Pre-Seed to Series B founders I talk to start the ATS evaluation looking for a tool. The right way to look at it is this: you're picking a system to run a process. The process is finding senior people who can build your company in a sector where the talent is small, hard to find, and being pursued by five other categories of employer at the same time.

The ATS supports the process. It doesn't replace it. The combination that actually moves senior hires forward at this stage is a sector specialist on the recruiting side, a platform that captures the actual signal of senior hires (operational scale, exit history, sector tenure, real career arc), and a transparency loop that keeps the founder in the room without burning founder hours on candidate review.

If that combination sounds like Filament plus VoltForce, it's because Filament was built for it. If you're at Pre-Seed to Series B in climatech, advanced materials, or Industry 4.0 and you're picking an ATS this quarter, the conversation is worth having.

Frequently Asked

ATS choice at early stage, answered.

What's the best ATS for a Pre-Seed startup?
At Pre-Seed, most startups don't need a paid ATS yet. Hiring 1 to 3 people typically happens through warm intros, so a Notion doc, a Google Sheet, or Filament's standalone tier is enough. The right time to upgrade is when hiring volume justifies the workflow overhead, usually at Seed or early Series A.
Is Greenhouse worth it for a Series A startup?
Usually not. Greenhouse is built for enterprise hiring volume and the pricing reflects that. For Series A startups hiring 10 to 25 people across the year, lighter options like Filament standalone or a basic Lever tier usually fit better. Greenhouse starts to make sense once you have a dedicated TA team and are hiring 50+ people per year.

For Pre-Seed to Series B teams, the practical difference is small. Both are enterprise ATS platforms priced and configured for larger orgs.
